# Participation and operational risks

Cannabis is preparing mainnet launch. The release must identify the canonical chain, contracts, founder beneficiary, ceremony witness policy, claim opening and verification policy.

Markets, liquidity, exchange acceptance and community adoption are uncertain. Prices can fall substantially. Founder vesting constrains release timing but does not eliminate the founder's economic conflict or off-chain arrangements.

Person verification depends on honest independent ceremony witnesses and continuous in-person attendance. A colluding quorum, compromised witness keys, substitution or exclusion can undermine access and fairness. Recurring rounds allow later newcomers, but durable cross-round human verification remains unfinished: a new tag and new secret can hide a returning person from cryptographic checks. Each venue submits one final roster per round. Missing a round permits consideration in a later round; losing every identity backup can permanently prevent claiming. The initial claim minimum is configurable, defaults to one and is disclosed in the reviewed release. Deployment has no recruitment quota. The founder wallet may later raise the minimum only up to current enrolled membership, without revoking claims or suspending eligible members; loss of that wallet prevents further increases. Every claimant still needs genuine verification; a commitment count cannot prove distinct humanity. The supply supports up to 990,000 claimants.

Zero-knowledge membership omits the enrolled identity from the proof, but a singleton group makes its sole member identifiable as the claimant. Small groups have limited privacy; participants can choose to wait for more real members. Wallet addresses, balances, claim nullifiers, transfers and transaction metadata remain public. Timing, RPC logs, ceremony records and venue batches, reused wallets, malware and small effective anonymity sets can undermine privacy.

Proof generation and verification require useful computation; ordinary transactions incur network fees. Enrollment updates can require proof regeneration. A failed transaction can still consume gas. The project does not claim zero electricity, carbon neutrality or a measured per-transaction environmental footprint.

Custom contracts, integrations and operations require independent review. Software bugs, compromised dependencies, loss of wallet or identity secrets, chain or RPC failures, and registrar compromise can cause irreversible loss. Immutable contracts have no emergency recovery or privileged balance reversal.

Legal classification, privacy/data-protection obligations, tax, branding, marketing and listing requirements need qualified review. Cannabis conveys no cannabis-product entitlement, health claim or affiliation with an existing cannabis business or currency.
